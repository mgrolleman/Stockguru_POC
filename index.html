Play with data

A few real examples to get a feel for how different investment types have actually moved over the last years.

These examples are ordered roughly from lower risk — broad and diversified — to higher risk, ending with a single stock and a single cryptocurrency.

All World ETF — last 5 years, monthly (indexed to 100)

The All World ETF is a fund that invests in thousands of stocks worldwide at once, spread across dozens of countries.

You can see steady growth over the last 5 years, with a clear dip in 2022 as interest rates rose and inflation fears spread.

Illustrative monthly figures, indexed to 100 at the start. Not real-time data.
S&P 500 — last 5 years, monthly

The S&P 500 is an index of 500 large U.S. companies, often used as a benchmark for the American stock market.

You can see strong growth, largely driven by tech stocks, with a clear dip in 2022 when interest rates rose sharply.

Illustrative monthly averages, not real-time data. Source: S&P Dow Jones Indices, via Yahoo Finance.
Gold — last 5 years, monthly (USD/oz)

Gold is a precious metal many investors use as a hedge against inflation and uncertainty, rather than for growth.

You can see a strong rise, especially from 2024 onward, as uncertainty and high inflation pushed investors toward safe havens.

Illustrative monthly figures, not real-time data. Source: Macrotrends, JM Bullion.
Netflix — last 5 years, monthly (individual stock)

Netflix is a single company's stock, not a spread-out fund like the examples above — so its price moves entirely on its own story.

You can see it climb hard — but also crash over 70% in 2022 after a shock subscriber loss, before rallying over 600% to a new high, then dropping again. A single well-known stock can swing far harder than a diversified fund.

This is just one example of a well-known stock — chosen to show that even famous, popular companies can also drop hard, not only rise.

Illustrative monthly figures, adjusted for Netflix's November 2025 stock split. Not real-time data.
Bitcoin — last 5 years, monthly (USD)

Bitcoin is a cryptocurrency with no underlying company or government, whose value rests entirely on supply, demand, and belief. It's the best-known cryptocurrency by far — but there are over 10,000 different cryptocurrencies out there.

You can see huge swings — sharp peaks like late 2024, but also steep drops, like the 2022 "crypto winter".

Illustrative monthly figures, not real-time data. Source: CoinGecko, SoFi, Official Data.

New: what if scenario's

Beyond the historical charts above, try your own hypothetical investment below and see what it could be worth today.

These are the questions you can answer:

What if I bought an ETF, stock, gold or crypto? What if I bought once at a specific moment? What if I bought a fixed amount per month? What would be the gain or loss today?

  1. Step 1: choose an investment type
  2. Step 2: choose when you bought, and when you sold (default: today)
  3. Step 3: choose a buying strategy
  4. Step 4: view the return you could have earned

"Buy once" assumes a single lump-sum purchase made on your chosen buy date, held until your chosen sell date. "Buy monthly (DCA)" means dollar-cost averaging — investing a fixed amount every month across that same period instead of all at once, something many beginner investors do (as covered in the quiz), since it smooths out the effect of timing. The examples above use €1,000 as a placeholder — since we're looking at percentage return, the actual amount you'd invest doesn't change the result.

Want to know more about DCA? It's explained in the "Building a portfolio" section of the quiz.
