How AppName works

AppName takes you on a journey: set goals, learn through videos, test your knowledge with a quiz, play with actual data, and use the guide to build a shortlist of interesting stock investments.

Every section has three quiz levels — Beginner, Intermediate, and Advanced (Advanced is still coming) — each a bit harder than the last.

AppName does not provide financial advice. It's built for education only.

A few real examples to get a feel for how different investment types have actually moved over the last years.

These examples are ordered roughly from lower risk — broad and diversified — to higher risk, ending with a single stock and a single cryptocurrency.

This tool includes the 10 largest individual stocks, the 3 largest cryptocurrencies, and the 3 largest ETFs, alongside gold and silver.

All World ETF

The All World ETF is a fund that invests in thousands of stocks worldwide at once, spread across dozens of countries.

Steady growth over the last 5 years, with a clear dip in 2022 as interest rates rose and inflation fears spread.

S&P 500

The S&P 500 is an index of 500 large U.S. companies, often used as a benchmark for the American stock market.

Strong growth, largely driven by tech stocks, with a clear dip in 2022 when interest rates rose sharply.

Gold

Gold is a precious metal many investors use as a hedge against inflation and uncertainty, rather than for growth.

A strong rise, especially from 2024 onward, as uncertainty and high inflation pushed investors toward safe havens.

Netflix

Netflix is a single company's stock, not a spread-out fund like the examples above — so its price moves entirely on its own story.

A hard climb, but also a crash of over 70% in 2022 after a shock subscriber loss, before rallying over 600% to a new high, then dropping again. A single well-known stock can swing far harder than a diversified fund.

This is just one example of a well-known stock — chosen to show that even famous, popular companies can also drop hard, not only rise.

Bitcoin

Bitcoin is a cryptocurrency with no underlying company or government, whose value rests entirely on supply, demand, and belief. It's the best-known cryptocurrency by far — but there are over 10,000 different cryptocurrencies out there.

Huge swings — sharp peaks like late 2024, but also steep drops, like the 2022 "crypto winter".

Want to understand the difference between ETFs, stocks, gold, and crypto in more depth? Check the "Investment types" section in the quiz.

Curious why prices move the way they do? That's covered in the "How markets move" section.

Simulations

Beyond the historical charts above, try your own hypothetical investment below and see what it could be worth today.

Both lines indexed to 100 at the start, so they can be compared directly.

These are the questions you can answer:

What if I bought an ETF, stock, gold or crypto? What if I bought once at a specific moment? What if I bought a fixed amount per month? What would be the gain or loss today?

  1. Step 1: choose an investment type
  2. Step 2: choose when you bought, and when you sold (default: today)
  3. Step 3: choose a buying strategy
  4. Step 4: view the return you could have earned

"Buy once" assumes a single lump-sum purchase made on your chosen buy date, held until your chosen sell date. "Buy monthly (DCA)" means dollar-cost averaging — investing your chosen amount every month across that same period instead of all at once, something many beginner investors do (as covered in the quiz), since it smooths out the effect of timing.

Want to know more about DCA? It's explained in the "Building a portfolio" section of the quiz.
