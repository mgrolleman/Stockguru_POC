How Stockguru works

Every section has three quiz levels — Beginner, Intermediate, and Advanced (Advanced is still coming) — each a bit harder than the last.

Each section also has an optional summary you can read, or just listen to, if you want the fuller story behind it.

You earn points as you go, and once every Beginner section is done, Intermediate unlocks — and next, Advanced.

Play with data has two goals: see how different investment types actually moved over the last years, and try your own what-if scenario to see what return you might have earned.

Play with data

A few real examples to get a feel for how different investment types have actually moved over the last years.

All World ETF — last 5 years, monthly (indexed to 100)

A global stock ETF like this spreads your money across thousands of companies in dozens of countries, so it rides broad global growth rather than any single market's swings.

Illustrative monthly figures, indexed to 100 at the start. Not real-time data.
S&P 500 — last 5 years, monthly

The S&P 500 tracks 500 of the largest U.S. companies, and ETFs that follow it are often seen as lower-risk than picking individual stocks since your money is spread across all 500 at once.

Illustrative monthly averages, not real-time data. Source: S&P Dow Jones Indices, via Yahoo Finance.
Gold — last 5 years, monthly (USD/oz)

Gold doesn't pay dividends or interest, but it has held or grown its value through the same stretch — which is why many investors use it as a hedge rather than a growth engine.

Illustrative monthly figures, not real-time data. Source: Macrotrends, JM Bullion.
Bitcoin — last 5 years, monthly (USD)

Bitcoin swings far more sharply than the other three in this list — big rallies, deep drops — which is exactly the trade-off between higher potential return and higher risk.

Illustrative monthly figures, not real-time data. Source: CoinGecko, SoFi, Official Data.

New: what if scenario's

Beyond the historical charts above, try your own hypothetical investment below and see what it could be worth today.

What if I bought an ETF, stock, gold or crypto? What if I bought once at a specific moment? What if I bought a fixed amount per month? What would be the gain or loss today?

Track a real trade

Log a buy or sell for a month, and check back later to see what return it would have earned.

"Buy once" assumes a single lump-sum purchase made at the very start of your selected time horizon, held until today. "Buy monthly (DCA)" means dollar-cost averaging — investing a fixed amount every month instead of all at once, something many beginner investors do (as covered in the quiz), since it smooths out the effect of timing. The examples above use €1,000 as a placeholder — since we're looking at percentage return, the actual amount you'd invest doesn't change the result.
